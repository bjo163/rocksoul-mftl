# MFTL Research Policy

## Mission

Document world mythology, folklore, supernatural claims, historical ritual practices, and related traditions with traceable provenance, then permit explicit comparative analysis without collapsing description into judgment.

## Source priority

Prefer, in order:

1. primary texts, inscriptions, or archaeological evidence;
2. peer-reviewed scholarship and university-press works;
3. museums, libraries, archives, and recognized academic reference works;
4. institutional sources;
5. lower-authority sources only as discovery leads.

Do not use AI-generated pages as evidence.

## Mandatory provenance

Every accepted record must preserve:

- source title;
- author when known;
- date when known;
- source type;
- stable locator/URL/identifier;
- access time for web sources;
- which claims/practices the source supports.

## Cross-check rule

Do not publish a strong historical claim from a single weak secondary source. For disputed claims, preserve disagreement and lower confidence instead of forcing consensus.

## Theological assessment

The theological layer is optional and separate from academic classification.

Normative comparison may reference:

- Qur'an
- Torah
- Psalms
- Gospel

The corpus must store the cited passage/reference and the relationship to the assessed claim. Do not silently infer a verdict from a tradition name alone.

## Human dignity / anti-targeting rule

Never classify a living person, ethnicity, nationality, or entire population as `shirk`, `mushrik`, evil, inferior, or a target.

Assessment codes describe **claim patterns and documented practices**. A historical tradition may contain several conflicting interpretations.

## Automation rule

Automated runs may discover and draft records. They may not fabricate citations or upgrade uncertain evidence to certainty. When evidence is insufficient, mark `needs_review: true`.
